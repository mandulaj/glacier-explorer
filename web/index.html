Base map

Terrain from Mapterhorn (swisstopo, BEV, IGN and others); places and borders from OpenStreetMap via OpenFreeMap. Turn the shading down to zero for a plain black-and-white map. Seabed from EMODnet Bathymetry around the Alps and coarser global terrain tiles elsewhere; the sea follows global mean sea level (Spratt & Lisiecki 2016), so past shorelines are indicative: local land movement is not included.

Since 1850

Switzerland only. Glacier outlines from the Swiss Glacier Inventories of 1850, 1931, 1973, 2010 and 2016 (GLAMOS, CC BY 4.0). Between two inventories the earlier outline fades into the later one, and the area is interpolated: glaciers did not shrink that evenly. Historic national maps (from 1844) and aerial photographs (from 1926, the latest six years of flights shown together) are served by swisstopo. The terrain is today's throughout. "Lost ice in 3D" puts back the ice that stood above it: the reconstructed glacier surface of about 1850 (Reinthaler & Paul 2025). Only 1850 is data: towards today that ice is thinned in step with the area loss inside the inventory outlines. After 2025 the bar runs on to 2100 with a model projection, so far only for the Great Aletsch Glacier and the glaciers connected to it: our own runs of the Instructed Glacier Model with glacier data from OGGM, under two idealised temperature paths. They are not calibrated against the measured retreat and are not published projections: read them as an illustration of the mechanism. The thinning ice sinks below today's terrain, so it is shown as colour on the ground instead of in 3D.

Ice

Ice from a PISM simulation at 1 km resolution, Seguinot et al. 2018. Click the map for the ice history of a place, the source of its ice, or a cross-section. It is a model result, not a reconstruction: thickness is likely overestimated, and the present-day state is not today's glaciers. Erosion is a relative potential, and debris sources are assigned by nearest massif. Zoom out for the ice sheet over northern Europe. It is not part of the simulation: flat outlines from geological reconstructions, every thousand years from 25 to 10 ka (DATED-1, Hughes et al. 2016), coarser before, and before 38 ka one outline per climate stage (Batchelor et al. 2019), faded into each other in between.

Whole Alps, 1 km. PISM simulation of the whole glacial cycle, 120 000 years to today, driven by a scaled ice-core temperature record (Seguinot et al. 2018; data). Has every view: flow, sliding, bed temperature, erosion, debris. Its ice is likely too thick.

Switzerland, 500 m. The Swiss Time Machine simulation with the IGM model (data), built on Leger et al. 2025, a model tuned to field evidence. Finer and thinner ice, but only Switzerland and 37 to 7 ka (marked on the time bar), and published as a visualisation, not as scientific results. It comes with surface speed only: the flow direction shown is derived here from the slope of the ice surface. Sliding, bed temperature, erosion and debris stay with the 1 km model, and flow traces and cross-sections end at the edge of its area.
